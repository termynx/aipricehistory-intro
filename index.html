A sale price only means something next to the prices before it.

Amazon and Flipkart prices move constantly. A product can go up in September and come back down in October with a "limited-time deal" badge attached, and the badge will be technically true. The strike-through price is rarely what the product sold for last week.

A price history fixes that by showing the listed price at each check over the past months. With it, three questions get a quick answer: is this actually low for this product, how often does it drop, and is it worth waiting.

AIPriceHistory keeps this record for Amazon products in 22 countries and for Flipkart in India, in each store's own currency. Looking up a product needs no account; a free account is used only to set alerts.

How it works

The whole process is three steps, and the first one is the only one that takes effort.

  1. Paste a product link or search by nameAny Amazon link works, including short amzn.to and a.co links, and Flipkart links for India. Go to aipricehistory.com and paste it in.
  2. Read the historyThe page shows the lowest and highest recorded prices, a month-by-month chart, the date of the last check, and a rating of today's price against the past.
  3. Set an alert if the price is not right yetChoose a target price and get told by email, browser notification, or the Android app when the listing reaches it. Alerts live in the price tracker.

Reading a price rating

Each product page compares today's price with its recorded range. The two situations below are the ones worth recognising.

₹5,999

Recorded range ₹5,999 – ₹11,990

At the lowest recorded price. Buying now is reasonable.

$249

Recorded range $179 – $279

Above the usual price despite a "deal" label. Worth setting an alert at $189.

Questions people ask first

Is it free?

Yes. Looking up price history needs no account and there is no subscription. A free account is only needed to save alerts.

Where do the prices come from?

From AIPriceHistory's own checks of the public product page, roughly once a day. The chart shows the listed price at each check. Coupons, bank offers and exchange values are not included, and a drop that lasts only a few hours can be missed.

Does it predict future prices?

No. The rating compares today's price with past prices for the same listing. It is a record, not a forecast; past drops suggest a pattern but do not guarantee one.

Which stores are covered?

Amazon in 22 countries, including amazon.in, amazon.com, amazon.co.uk, amazon.de and amazon.ae, and Flipkart in India. The country list above links to each store's page.

Can I use it without leaving Amazon?

Yes, with the browser extension. It adds the price history below the product title on Amazon product pages, so there is nothing to copy or paste.
